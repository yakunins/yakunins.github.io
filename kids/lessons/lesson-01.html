Theory

History: From ARPANET to the Modern Internet

The Internet started as a military experiment in the late 1960s called ARPANET. The US Department of Defense wanted a way for computers to communicate even if part of the network was destroyed. By 1969, four universities were connected — and the first message ever sent was “LO” (the system crashed before they could finish “LOGIN”!).

Over the next decades, more networks joined and adopted shared rules (protocols). In 1991, Tim Berners-Lee released the World Wide Web — and the Internet as we know it was born.

Internet vs. the Web — What’s the Difference?

People often use “Internet” and “Web” as if they mean the same thing. They don’t:

  • The Internet is the global network of connected computers — the physical cables, routers, and protocols that allow data to travel around the world.
  • The Web (World Wide Web) is just one service that runs on the Internet — it’s the system of websites and web pages you access through a browser.

Think of it this way: the Internet is the road system, and the Web is the delivery trucks driving on those roads. Email, online gaming, and video calls also use the Internet — but they are not “the Web.”

How Devices Connect: ISPs, Routers, Cables, Wi-Fi

When you open a website on your phone or laptop, here’s what happens behind the scenes:

  1. Your device connects to your home router (via Wi-Fi or cable).
  2. The router connects to your ISP (Internet Service Provider) — the company you pay for Internet access.
  3. Your ISP connects to a huge network of undersea cables, fiber optics, and satellites that span the globe.
  4. The request reaches the server where the website is stored, and the server sends the data back the same way.

Key Concept: The Internet Is a Network of Networks

There’s no single “Internet headquarters.” The Internet is made up of millions of smaller networks — home networks, school networks, company networks, government networks — all connected together. That’s what makes it so resilient: if one path breaks, data can find another way through.
